MATRIX · PRIVACY

Privacy

Short version: this site has no accounts and no ads, sets a cookie only if you ask it to remember a like, and only counts visits if you click Allow. Your camera, microphone and photos never leave your device. It keeps a few settings in your own browser, and the Live board fetches weather and GitHub data from three public APIs.

LAST UPDATED 2026-11-04

Who runs this

Matrix is a personal, non-commercial project by Yash Mahajan. The source code is public at github.com/YashM20/dot-matrix. There is nothing to sign in to: the site is a set of static files, and everything runs in your browser. The one exception is likes: when they are switched on and you like something, a small database keeps the like.

What the site collects

Nothing personal. There are no cookies (unless you ask to be remembered when you like something), no advertising, no fingerprinting and no third-party fonts or CDNs (the typeface is served from this site). Analytics is off unless you turn it on, as the next section explains. The page's Content Security Policy only allows connections to this site and to the APIs listed below.

Analytics

The site can count visits with PostHog, but only if you say yes. On your first visit a small box in the corner asks. Until you click Allow, nothing is loaded, sent or stored for it. No thanks keeps it off, and the box doesn't come back. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, analytics stays off and the box never shows.

After you click Allow, the site sends:

  • Page views: the address of each page you open, cut down to its path and the board or card it shows. Programs and drawings packed into a link (the p= and d= parts) and everything else after the ? are removed first.
  • A few actions, by name: a card's buttons (such as LINK or PNG), copying or sharing a link, changing the board, theme or display skin, and which palette or shell command ran. Only a command's name is sent, never what you typed.
  • What any analytics tool sees: your browser and system type, screen size, language, time zone and the site you came from (just its domain). PostHog also receives your IP address with each request, as any server does.

There are no accounts, so the data isn't tied to a name or an email. PostHog gives your browser a random id so visits from the same browser count together. The data is kept on PostHog's US cloud, under PostHog's privacy policy.

The requests go to /ingest on this site, and Vercel passes them on to PostHog. So the page never connects to another domain for analytics, and no PostHog script is loaded from anywhere else.

To turn it off: on a board page, open the command palette (⌘K or Ctrl+K) and pick "Analytics: off", or on a phone switch off ANALYTICS in TOOLS. Sending stops at once, the random id is forgotten, and what PostHog stored in your browser is deleted. Turning on Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track stops it too.

What is stored on your device

Some settings and the things you make are saved in your browser's localStorage for this site, so they survive a reload. Apart from PostHog's id (under Analytics below), every key stays on your device and is never sent anywhere. The site works without any of them, and most are only written once you change a setting or make something.

Settings

dot480:theme
The colour scheme, accent and contrast you picked, and for a custom accent its hue and the colours made from it.
dot480:share-theme
Whether links you copy carry your theme.
dot480:cards
The card size you picked (large, medium or small).
dot480:skin
The display skin you picked (OLED, CRT, e-ink and so on).
dot480:pro
Pro mode is on.
dot480:flags
Feature switches you turned on or off in this browser, such as the flip-disc clack sound.
dot480:clack, dot480:worker
Older copies of two of those switches. They are still read, and removed the next time you change the switch.
dot480:intro
You closed the one-line intro above the boards.
dot480:fold:…
Which "More" panels in MAKE you left open or closed, one key per panel.
dot480:likes
Only if you like something: your answer to "Remember this browser?", the last day a remembered browser checked in, and what you liked here, so your like shows at once. See Likes.
dot480:analytics
Your answer to the analytics box: on or off. Never written if your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track.
dot480:pond
Only if you turn on POND MEMORY: the day the pond was first made, the day of your last visit and a visit count, so the pond can age. Turning it off deletes it.

Things you make

dot480.dots.gallery
Up to 40 DOTS programs you ran or remixed in MAKE · CODE.
dot480.studio.v1, dot480.studio.library
The card you are composing in MAKE · COMPOSE, and up to 24 cards you saved there.
dot480.editor.v1
The drawing board's document, saved as you work.
dot480.editor.clip
The last frames or layers you copied in the drawing board, so you can paste them in another tab.
dot480.editor.links
The last 40 drawing board links you made or opened: their id, name, size and time, so the board can show where a remix came from.
dot480.editor.sheet
On phones, how tall you left each of the drawing board's bottom panels.
dot480.hive.scores.v1
The game's high-score table: three-letter initials and scores.

Live data

dot480:live:…
The last weather, GitHub contributions and now-playing readings the Live board showed, one key per source, so it can start from them next time. They are ignored after 7 days.

Analytics

ph_…_posthog, __ph_opt_in_out_…
Only after you click Allow: PostHog's random id for this browser, the current visit and your opt-in. The id is sent with each event. Switching analytics off deletes them.

Two keys live in sessionStorage instead, which your browser clears when you close the tab:

dot480:tilt
You allowed tilt on this tab, so tilt-reactive cards don't ask again until you close it.
dot480:shell-history
The last 100 commands you typed in the dot shell on this tab, so ↑ finds them after you close and reopen it.

Open tabs of the site also talk to each other over a BroadcastChannel (so clocks and live cards stay in step); those messages never leave your browser and aren't stored.

To clear it all: remove this site's data in your browser settings (usually under site settings or "cookies and site data" for matrix.reactopia.me), or in developer tools under Application → Local storage and Session storage. In a private window it is gone when you close the window.

Likes

Likes may not be switched on yet. When they are, widgets and gallery programs get a like button, and counts are public. Nobody can see who liked what. If you like something:

  • What is sent. The widget's id, or the program itself so the server can check it and work out its key. The program is not stored, only a hash of it.
  • Your network, hashed. The server reads your IP address only to work out your network (the first three parts of an IPv4 address, or the first four groups of an IPv6 address). It stores a keyed hash of that network, never the address, and keeps it for up to 30 days to apply limits and stop spam.
  • Remember or just this once. The first time, you're asked whether to remember this browser. If you choose Remember, the server sets one cookie, dot480_lk, for one year. Scripts on the page can't read it, and it is only sent to the likes API. It holds a random id, the day it was issued and how many days you came back, signed so it can't be changed. It is used for likes and nothing else. If you choose Just this once, or your browser sends Global Privacy Control, no cookie is set: your like counts a little less and is tied to your network's hash for 30 days.
  • What is kept. For each like: a keyed hash of the cookie's id (or of your network), the time and whether it counts yet. An unliked like is kept as removed, so a mistake or abuse can be undone.
  • Forget this browser. The control sits under the like button once you chose Remember. It removes every like this browser made while remembered, recounts those items, and deletes the cookie and the dot480:likes key. Likes made just this once can't be tied to you, and their record ends with the 30-day network hash.
  • Who handles it. Likes are stored in Upstash Redis (set up through Vercel), which processes them for this site under its own privacy policy. To keep bots out, the like button loads Vercel BotID, which runs a check in your browser and sends the result with the like.

Camera, microphone and motion

Some cards can use your device's sensors. The camera and microphone are only requested after you press the card's control, and your browser asks you first:

  • Camera (the mirror cards): frames from the front camera are drawn into the page and turned into dots on the spot. They are not recorded, saved or uploaded, and the camera stops when you switch it off or leave the tab.
  • Microphone (the audio board): the sound is analysed in the page by the browser's audio engine to move the dots. It never plays through your speakers and is not uploaded.
  • Motion and tilt (tilt-reactive cards on phones): orientation readings only move the dots on screen.
  • Gamepad (the game): button presses are read while you play.

The site never asks for your location. If you use a REC or export button, the clip or image (a REC clip can include the board's sound) is made in your browser and saved to your device as a download; it is not uploaded. You can revoke any permission in your browser's site settings at any time.

Photos and files you open

An image you drop, paste or pick on the home page's touchable card, a photo you drop into "Your photo as dots" or use as a reference in DRAW, and any JSON card or drawing you open, is read and processed inside your browser. It is not uploaded anywhere, and the home card keeps nothing once you leave the page.

Share links

When you share a DOTS program, the program itself is packed into the link (the p= part of the address), and a drawing board link packs its frames the same way (the d= part). Nothing is stored on a server: anyone with the link can read the program, it ends up in browser history like any address, and the request for it appears in the hosting logs described below. Don't put anything private in a program you share. Copy buttons write to your clipboard only when you press them.

Third-party services

Apart from analytics, which goes through this site's /ingest address and only after you allow it, only the Live board talks to other services, only while it is on screen, and the requests come straight from your browser. Like any website, each service sees your IP address and browser type when it is contacted.

  • Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com) receives the coordinates to forecast, rounded to two decimals. These are Tokyo's by default, or the lat / lon you put in the address; the site does not read or send your location. See Open-Meteo's terms.
  • GitHub contributions (github-contributions-api.jogruber.de, a community service, not GitHub) receives the GitHub username whose public contribution calendar is shown: the author's by default, or the gh you put in the address.
  • GitHub (api.github.com) receives the same username, only if the service above fails, to read that user's public activity. See GitHub's privacy statement.

Links to other sites (GitHub, the essay's references) are ordinary links; once you follow one, that site's own policy applies. The site tells them only that you came from this site, not which page.

Hosting

The site is hosted by Vercel. Like any web host, Vercel processes the technical details of each request (such as IP address, browser user agent, the address requested and the time) to deliver and protect the site, under Vercel's privacy policy. This project does not turn on Vercel's analytics and does not use those logs to identify visitors.

The MCP server and scripts

The mcp/ server and the scripts in the repository are not part of the website. They only run if you clone the repository and start them on your own computer; the MCP server talks to your AI client over stdio, and its push_to_device tool only sends to a local (loopback) address you configure.

Children

The site isn't aimed at children. It doesn't collect personal information from anyone, children included.

Changes

If what the site stores or sends changes, this page changes with it and the date above moves. Every past version is in the repository's history.
